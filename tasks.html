Task in BPMN

Tasks in BPMN represent specific actions or units of work performed during a process. They are the fundamental building blocks of a BPMN diagram and should be designed to focus on a single, well-defined responsibility.

Tasks are typically represented as rectangles in BPMN diagrams and can include manual, automated, or user-driven activities.

  • Use verb-noun pairs for clarity and consistency (e.g., "Approve Invoice" or "Send Email").
  • Limit scope — ensure each task represents one logical action to avoid confusion.
  • Document inputs, outputs, and responsible roles in the attribute sections to provide context and accountability.
  • Always make sure One BPMN should not have more than 15 Tasks so that the process is readable

Important Things to Note

  • Tasks should be actionable and clearly defined to avoid ambiguity.
  • Ensure tasks are sequenced logically to reflect the actual flow of the process.
  • Use consistent naming conventions across all tasks to maintain clarity.
  • Tasks always have 1 Incoming Arrow and 1 Outgoing Arrow!

Collapsed Processes

What is a Collapsed Process?

  • A collapsed process, also known as a sub-process, encapsulates related activities into a single, manageable unit.
  • It hides the internal details of the process, showing only its name and boundary.
  • Collapsed processes are represented by a rectangle with a small plus sign, indicating that more details are available.

When to Use Collapsed Processes

  • To reduce visual complexity in the main process diagram.
  • When the internal details are not immediately relevant to the audience.
  • To improve maintainability and focus on high-level process flow.
  • For processes that are reused across multiple diagrams.

Benefits & Best Practices

  • Reduces visual complexity
  • Improves maintainability
  • Enables process reuse
  • Better process organization
  • Use meaningful names
  • Keep related activities together
  • Document boundaries
  • Consider detail level needed

Expanded Processes

What is an Expanded Process?

An expanded process shows all the details of a subprocess directly within the main process diagram. It's useful when you want to display the internal workflow without having to navigate to a separate diagram.

This approach provides immediate visibility into subprocess activities, making it easier to understand the overall process flow at a glance.

When to Use Expanded Process

  • When immediate visibility of subprocess details is important
  • For relatively simple subprocesses that don't overwhelm the main diagram
  • Also Triggering Other Flows If Certain Conditions are Met During a Subprocess Stage
